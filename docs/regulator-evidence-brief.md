# Regulator-evidence brief

> Cited research from the SPEC swarm. Only verified mappings are asserted; the rest stay pending.

## Cross-cutting evidence principles
- Integrity / tamper-evidence: every regime demands that records be protected against undetected alteration or deletion. SOC 2 CC7.1 (file-integrity / change-detection) and CC2.1 ('verifiable, protected'); FDA 21 CFR 11.10(e) ('record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information' = append-only) and 11.10(a) ('discern invalid or altered records'); ISO 27001 A.8.15 (which names 'cryptographic hashing' and 'append-only recording' as log-protection techniques) and A.5.28 (records 'complete and unaltered', chain-of-custody); GDPR Art. 32(1)(b) ('integrity ... of processing systems') and Art. 5(1)(f). The SHA-256 hash chain is the common load-bearing mechanism.
- Attributability / actor identification: each regime requires that recorded actions be tied to an identified actor. SOC 2 CC6.1 explicitly extends identification to 'software' actors (covering AI agents) and CC1.5 holds individuals accountable; FDA 11.10(j) (accountability for actions under one's signature); ISO 27001 A.8.15 (per-event user/actor ID) + Clause 5.3 (assigned responsibilities); GDPR Art. 5(2) accountability. Per-change agent-vs-human attribution is the cross-cutting fit, and distinguishing AI agent from human is the recurring AI-governance angle.
- Contemporaneity / time-sequencing: records must be time-stamped and ordered as events occur. FDA 11.10(e) (computer-generated, time-stamped audit trail) and 11.10(k) (time-sequenced development/modification trail); SOC 2 CC8.1 ('Tracks System Changes' prior to implementation); ISO 27001 A.8.15 (when/timestamp). The chain's monotonic sequence + timestamps satisfy this.
- Completeness / detection-and-logging of risk-relevant events: regimes expect that anomalous, security-relevant, or vulnerability-introducing changes are detected and logged. SOC 2 CC7.2 ('logging of unusual system activities', anomaly analysis) and CC7.1 (detect 'changes that introduce new vulnerabilities'); ISO 27001 A.8.28 secure coding + A.8.29 security testing (scan every change); GDPR Art. 32(1)(d) ('regularly testing, assessing and evaluating the effectiveness' of measures). Continuous grounded scanning of every change is the common mechanism.
- Confidentiality of the record itself / no new exposure: the audit record must not itself become a data-leak vector. SOC 2 CC8.1 'Protects Confidential Information' + CC6.1 'Restricts Logical Access' to data at rest; FDA 11.10(c) record protection; ISO 27001 A.8.12 DLP + A.8.11 data masking; GDPR Art. 5(1)(f) + Art. 25 data-minimisation-by-design. Pre-storage secret redaction is the shared control, and it is especially load-bearing precisely because the chain is append-only/un-deletable.
- Retention / availability / restorability: records must survive and be retrievable for a defined period. SOC 2 CC2.1 ('retained') tested across the Type II window; FDA 11.10(c) (accurate, ready retrieval through the retention period); ISO 27001 Clauses 9.1/9.2.2 (retain audit/monitoring evidence) + A.8.10 (deletion per schedule); GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation + Art. 32(1)(c) (restore availability after an incident). This is the one cross-cutting principle the product does NOT yet satisfy  -  retention policy + integrity-checked backup/restore is PLANNED across all four real regimes.
- Retrievability / agency-readable export: regulators expect to obtain accurate, complete copies for inspection. FDA 11.10(b) (human-readable AND electronic copies suitable for agency copying); SOC 2 CC2.1 ('accessible'); GDPR Art. 30(3) (records 'in writing, including in electronic form') and Art. 30(4) (available to the supervisory authority). Partly met via report/dashboard output; a signed export-with-attestation bundle is the gap.
- No regime carries dedicated AI-specific controls in its core text: SOC 2 (TSP Section 100), FDA Part 11, and ISO 27001:2022 each contain ZERO AI clauses; AI-assisted development is governed by mapping onto existing controls, with AI-specific obligations living in adjacent instruments (FDA Jan-2025 draft AI guidance; ISO/IEC 42001:2023; EDPB Opinion 28/2024; EU AI Act). The correct posture is to run AI-agent changes through the same controls as human changes, which per-change attribution evidences.

## Recommendations
- TOP PRIORITY  -  Ship the retention policy + integrity-checked backup/restore (the single biggest cross-regime gap). It is required by SOC 2 CC2.1 'retained', FDA 11.10(c), ISO 27001 Clauses 9.1/9.2.2 + Annex A 8.10, and GDPR Art. 32(1)(c) + Art. 5(1)(e)  -  five distinct clauses across four regimes converge here. Make it a CONFIGURABLE, documented, consistently-enforced retention duration (SOC 2 leaves the number to the entity) with scheduled integrity-checked backups and a tested restore path that re-runs verify() on restore. Resolves the most 'still_pending' items of any single feature.
- SECOND  -  Build a signed evidence-export bundle with attestation: a self-contained export (human-readable Markdown + canonical JSON + the chain proof + a verify() result) that an auditor can copy and independently re-verify. Directly satisfies FDA 11.10(b) 'accurate and complete copies ... suitable for agency copying', strengthens GDPR Art. 30(3) electronic-form records, and turns the chain's integrity property into a portable artefact. Pairs naturally with the retention feature.
- THIRD  -  Add the storage-limitation / erasure story to reconcile append-only with GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) and ISO 27001 A.8.10: e.g. crypto-shredding or tombstoning of expired entries that preserves chain verifiability while making personal data unrecoverable after the retention window. This closes the one place where the append-only design is in tension with a regime (right-to-erasure), and should be designed alongside (not after) the retention feature.
- FOURTH  -  Research and map the EU AI Act from primary sources before any customer-facing claim. The supplied finding is a stub (example.com / 'test'). Per trust-but-verify, dispatch a verification pass against the Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689  -  especially Art. 12 (record-keeping/logging) and Art. 19 (automatically generated logs for high-risk systems), where the tamper-evident chain + attribution are a strong prima-facie fit. The EU AI Act is the highest-value AI-specific regime for this product and is currently a blank.
- FIFTH  -  Clarify scope boundaries in product/marketing materials: the product is an audit-trail-and-attribution EVIDENCE engine, not an IAM, e-signature, or validation-package vendor. Explicitly state that adopters must pair it with (a) IAM / authority checks for FDA 11.10(d)/(g) and 11.300, (b) a CSV / IQ-OQ-PQ validation package for 11.10(a), and (c) written SOPs for the accountability/change-approval policy layers (SOC 2 CC8.1 approval gate, FDA 11.10(j), ISO Clause 5.3). The existing compliance.py correctly lists Part 11 in PENDING_REGIMES  -  keep that honesty in customer-facing copy.
- SIXTH  -  When citing per-finding security frameworks (OWASP/CWE/GDPR), label them as a STRENGTHENING practice, not a literal clause requirement: ISO 27001 A.8.28 mandates scanning but does not name CVE/CWE/OWASP, and GDPR does not mandate a log/hash design. This keeps the evidence claims defensible under audit scrutiny and avoids over-asserting that a regime 'requires' the specific framework citations.
- SEVENTH (lower value, future)  -  Consider an optional electronic-signature linkage (FDA 11.70 + 11.50/11.200) and lightweight authority/role gating, for adopters who want the product to approach a fuller Part 11 posture without a separate IAM. This is a larger build and only worth it for regulated-life-sciences customers; the chain already provides the anti-transfer/binding property a signature would need.

## Still pending (AI regimes)
- EU AI Act SCOPE GATE (precondition, not a clause gap): All Art. 9/11/12/18/19/72 mappings bind this product ONLY IF the AI-assisted dev tooling (or a system built with it) is in scope as HIGH-RISK (Art. 6 + Annex III) or GPAI (Chapter V). An AI coding assistant is NOT automatically high-risk. The in-scope determination is an org/legal decision that must precede admitting any EU AI Act mapping to the verified catalog.
- EU AI Act Art. 18(1)  -  Provider keeps technical/QMS/notified-body docs + EU declaration of conformity for 10 YEARS. GAP: no enforced 10-year retention, no integrity-checked backup, no restore path (PLANNED); and the product holds development-change records, not the full Art. 17/47 document set Art. 18 enumerates. Obligates the PROVIDER.
- EU AI Act Art. 12(3)(a)-(d)  -  Biometric-system-specific logging (use period, reference DB, matched input data, human verifier identity). OUT OF SCOPE for an AI-dev governance tool unless the governed system is a biometric Annex III system; the product does not implement biometric-specific logging.
- EU AI Act effective-date watch: statutory baseline for the high-risk Section 3 obligations (Art. 12/18/19/72) is 2 Aug 2026 (Art. 113); Annex III classification from 2 Aug 2027. The 'Digital Omnibus on AI' (provisional agreement May 2026) would DEFER stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027  -  NOT yet law (medium confidence, pending formal adoption + OJ publication). GAP: the product feeds traceable change/scan evidence INTO the assessment but does not perform or produce the assessment artefact. - ISO 42001 Clause 7.5.3 + 9.1 + A.6.2.8  -  Retention, integrity-preserving storage, and restorability of event logs/documented information over the AIMS lifecycle. GAP: the PLANNED retention policy + integrity-checked backup/restore is unmet  -  the single largest cross-clause/cross-regime gap.
- NIST AI RMF GOVERN 6.1 + GenAI GV-6.1 family (third-party/supply-chain) and MAP 4.1 / MAP 4.2 (front-loaded scoping of third-party AI components incl. IP-infringement risk).
